Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Owensboro Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Water Next Door

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
