Kitchen Water Damage in Owensboro

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help Owensboro homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Toe Kick Space

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
